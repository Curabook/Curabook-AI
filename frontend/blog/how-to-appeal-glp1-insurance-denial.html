The 5-step appeal process

1

Get the denial in writing — immediately

Call your insurer and request the complete denial letter in writing. Ask them specifically: "What are the exact clinical criteria required for approval?" and "What specific criteria did I fail to meet?" Write down the names of everyone you speak with. This documentation becomes part of your appeal.

Also ask: "What is my appeal deadline?" Most insurers require appeals within 30-60 days of the denial date. Missing this deadline forfeits your right to internal appeal.

2

Gather your clinical evidence

Your appeal needs hard numbers. Collect everything:

  • Current BMI and documented weight history
  • HbA1c (current and trend — showing progression toward or into pre-diabetes/diabetes)
  • Fasting glucose values
  • Fasting lipid panel (especially if elevated triglycerides or LDL)
  • Blood pressure readings if hypertension present
  • Documentation of comorbidities: type 2 diabetes, pre-diabetes, PCOS, sleep apnea, hypertension, cardiovascular disease
  • History of prior weight loss attempts (programs, other medications, supervised plans)

3

Have your physician write a medical necessity letter

This is the single most important document in your appeal. The letter should include:

  • Your specific diagnosis codes (E66.01 for morbid obesity, E11 for type 2 diabetes, etc.)
  • Your current weight and BMI
  • Your relevant lab values
  • Why the medication is medically necessary for your specific situation
  • Why alternative treatments are insufficient or contraindicated
  • Reference to the insurer's own coverage criteria (available in their coverage determination document)
Tip: Bring a draft letter to your physician appointment. Most physicians don't have time to write these from scratch but will review and sign a well-prepared draft. Curabook PHI generates this draft automatically from your lab data.
4

Submit the complete appeal package

Your appeal package should include:

  • The denial letter (copy)
  • Your completed appeal form (from insurer's website)
  • Physician medical necessity letter
  • Lab results supporting medical necessity
  • Any relevant clinical guidelines (American Association of Clinical Endocrinology, Obesity Medicine Association)
  • A cover letter summarizing your case in 2-3 paragraphs

Submit via certified mail with return receipt, or through the insurer's online portal if available. Keep copies of everything.

5

Follow up, escalate, and use external review if needed

Follow up every 5-7 days after submission. Ask for the status and expected decision date. If the internal appeal is denied, you have the right to an independent external review — a third party reviews your case. External reviews overturn internal denials approximately 30-40% of the time for GLP-1 medications.

If all else fails, file a complaint with your state insurance commissioner. Insurers take state regulatory complaints seriously.

Common mistakes that sink appeals: Missing the deadline. Submitting without lab values. Generic letters that don't reference the insurer's specific criteria. Not escalating to external review after internal denial. Giving up after the first no.

Medicare and Medicaid appeals — different rules

Medicare: The GLP-1 Bridge Program (July 2026 - December 2027) provides $50/month access to approved GLP-1 medications for eligible Medicare beneficiaries with obesity. If denied under Medicare Advantage, the appeal process goes through your plan's grievance and appeals department. Medicare appeals have strict timelines (60 days for standard appeals, 72 hours for expedited).

Medicaid: California Medi-Cal removed GLP-1s for weight management in January 2026. Other state Medicaid programs vary significantly. If denied by Medicaid, you have the right to a fair hearing — an administrative law judge reviews your case. Medicaid fair hearing wins are common when the clinical case is well-documented.

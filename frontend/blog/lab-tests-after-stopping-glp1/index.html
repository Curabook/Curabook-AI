What Lab Tests to Get After Stopping Ozempic, Wegovy, or Mounjaro

The complete monitoring panel — which tests matter, what numbers signal a problem, and how often to check.

When you stop a GLP-1 medication, your metabolic markers begin shifting within weeks — often before you feel anything or see the scale move. The only way to know what's happening is blood work. But most people don't know which tests to order, what numbers constitute a problem, or when to get tested.

This guide answers all three questions.

The essential post-cessation panel — order this as one blood draw

Request this exact combination from your doctor or lab: Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP) + Lipid Panel + HbA1c + Fasting Insulin

This covers everything you need in a single fasting blood draw. Most labs process this within 1-3 days. Cost without insurance: approximately $50-120 at a direct-pay lab like Walk-In Lab or LabCorp Patient pricing.

🔴 HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)
Most important long-term marker — reflects average glucose over 90 days

HbA1c shows your average blood glucose over the past 2-3 months. On GLP-1 medications, HbA1c typically improves. After stopping, it begins reversing. The danger is that HbA1c has a 90-day lag — by the time it shows a significant rise, the underlying glucose problem has been developing for months.

Cliff signal threshold: Rise of 0.25% or more between two readings taken 6-8 weeks apart. A rise from 5.4% to 5.7% is already significant.

Normal: Below 5.7% | Pre-diabetes: 5.7-6.4% | Diabetes: 6.5%+

🔴 Fasting Insulin
The earliest cliff signal — often rises before glucose moves significantly

Fasting insulin is the most sensitive early warning marker for post-GLP-1 metabolic rebound. GLP-1 medications improve insulin sensitivity; when they stop, insulin resistance begins returning. The SURMOUNT-4 post-hoc analysis found fasting insulin rose approximately 26.3% in patients who regained significant weight — often detectable before HbA1c or fasting glucose showed major changes.

Cliff signal threshold: Any rising trend from your baseline, especially rising above 15 μIU/mL fasting. This is not included in standard CMP — request it specifically.

Optimal fasting insulin: Below 8 μIU/mL | Concerning: Above 15 μIU/mL

🟡 Fasting Glucose
Direct measure of current blood sugar control

Fasting glucose is included in the standard CMP. It reflects your glucose level after an overnight fast. After stopping GLP-1 medications, fasting glucose typically begins rising within 4-8 weeks as insulin resistance returns and the glucose-lowering effect of the medication fades.

Cliff signal threshold: Rise of 15% or more above your personal baseline reading. For example, if your baseline was 92 mg/dL, a reading of 106+ mg/dL is a cliff signal.

Normal: Below 100 mg/dL | Pre-diabetes: 100-125 mg/dL | Diabetes: 126+ mg/dL

🟡 Triglycerides
Indicates insulin resistance and metabolic stress returning

Triglycerides often improve significantly on GLP-1 therapy. After cessation, they begin rising as insulin resistance returns and dietary patterns shift. Elevated triglycerides (above 150 mg/dL) in combination with other markers indicate the insulin resistance triad — a key cliff signal pattern.

Cliff signal threshold: Rise of 20% or more above your baseline, or crossing 150 mg/dL.

Normal: Below 150 mg/dL | Borderline: 150-199 mg/dL | High: 200+ mg/dL

🟡 LDL Cholesterol
Cardiovascular risk marker that often improves on GLP-1 and reverses after stopping

LDL typically improves on GLP-1 medications as a downstream effect of weight loss and metabolic improvement. After stopping, LDL may rise, particularly if weight is regaining. For people who had elevated LDL pre-medication, monitoring is important.

Cliff signal threshold: Rise of 15% or more above your baseline.
🟢 HDL Cholesterol
The "good" cholesterol — typically rises with GLP-1 use and may fall after stopping

HDL improvement is one of the positive effects of GLP-1 therapy. A falling HDL after cessation, combined with rising triglycerides, is a classic metabolic syndrome pattern worth monitoring.

Watch for: HDL falling more than 10% below your on-medication baseline.

When to get tested — the monitoring schedule

Before stopping
Get a baseline draw while still on medication. This becomes your comparison point for all future tests.

Month 6

Ongoing
Every 3-6 months indefinitely, or as recommended by your provider.

The cliff signal pattern — what to watch for

Individual markers matter, but pattern recognition matters more. Three patterns are particularly significant:

Pattern 1: Insulin Resistance Triad — HbA1c rising + fasting glucose rising + triglycerides rising together. This combination indicates progressive insulin resistance return and is the classic GLP-1 cliff pattern.

Pattern 2: Early Warning — Fasting Insulin Rising Alone — Fasting insulin begins rising while glucose and HbA1c still look normal. This is the earliest detectable cliff signal, typically appearing 4-8 weeks post-cessation. Act here and you catch the problem before other markers deteriorate.

Pattern 3: Cardiovascular Cluster — LDL rising + HDL falling + triglycerides rising. Often accompanies weight regain and indicates worsening cardiometabolic health even if glucose markers are still stable.
